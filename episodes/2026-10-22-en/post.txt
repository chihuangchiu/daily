Title: 215 Years Ago Today, One Man Filled a Whole Concert | Franz Liszt

On October 22, 1811, Franz Liszt was born in the village of Raiding, in the Kingdom of Hungary; today the village is part of Austria. He made the piano the only star on the stage, and all of Europe went wild for him.

He showed an astonishing gift for music from early childhood. His family moved to Vienna, where he studied piano with Carl Czerny and composition with Antonio Salieri, and in 1823 they moved on to Paris.

Around the age of twenty, in Paris, he heard the violinist Niccolò Paganini play. That breathtaking technique shook him deeply. He went back to his piano and practised day and night, wanting to take the instrument somewhere no one had gone before. Many of the pieces he later wrote are still a great challenge for pianists today.

On June 9, 1840, in London, his performance was called a piano recital. Before that, almost no one had held a whole concert alone. He did it: one man and one piano were the whole stage.

He toured all of Europe, from Ireland to Russia, and wherever he went, audiences went wild. In 1844 the poet Heinrich Heine gave this craze a name: Lisztomania. He also gave much of his earnings to charity and to people in need. In 1847, at the height of his fame, he stopped touring.

In 1848 he settled in Weimar, Germany, as court music director. He created the symphonic poem, a story told by an orchestra. In 1850, in Weimar, he conducted the premiere of Richard Wagner's opera Lohengrin while Wagner was living in exile and could not be there. He used his own fame to open the way for someone else's music.

In his later years he gave more of his time to students. Young pianists came to his classes from everywhere, and he taught them without charging a fee. In 1865 he took minor holy orders, and people called him Abbé Liszt; in 1875 he became president of the music academy in Budapest. He died in Bayreuth on July 31, 1886, at the age of seventy-four.

Today, every piano recital still carries his shadow. He showed that one person and one instrument can fill a whole stage, and that talent can be used to light the way for others.
