Title: Strauss: 201 Years Ago Today, the Boy Banned From Music Became the Waltz King

On October 25, 1825, Johann Strauss II was born in Vienna. As a boy he was not allowed to make music. He grew up to become the Waltz King, and the whole world danced to his music.

His father, Johann Strauss I, was a famous musician in Vienna, but he did not want his son to follow him. He wanted the boy to become a banker. So the boy took violin lessons in secret. When his father found out, he reportedly whipped him. But the boy never put down the violin.

In October 1844, at the age of nineteen, he led his own orchestra for the first time, at Dommayer's Casino near Vienna. He wrote the music and conducted it himself, and soon all of Vienna knew his name.

On February 15, 1867, his waltz The Blue Danube was played for the first time in Vienna. It went on to travel all around the world, and it may be the most famous waltz ever written.

In 1872 he was invited to Boston, in the United States. At a huge music festival, the World's Peace Jubilee, he led more than a thousand performers in The Blue Danube. In his life he wrote about five hundred dance pieces, and operettas such as Die Fledermaus.

He died in Vienna on June 3, 1899, at the age of seventy-three. Today, Vienna's New Year's Concert still plays his waltzes. A boy banned from music set the whole world dancing.
