Title: Mahalia Jackson: 115 Years Ago Today, the Voice Behind "I Have a Dream" Was Born

On October 26, 1911, Mahalia Jackson was born in New Orleans. She became the Queen of Gospel, and the woman whose shout at the March on Washington helped Martin Luther King Jr. tell the world his dream.

Her family was poor, and she lost her mother very young. As a child she sang in church. At sixteen she moved north to Chicago. People kept telling her to sing the blues, but she always refused. To her, gospel was the music of hope.

In 1947 her gospel song "Move On Up a Little Higher" sold over a million copies, the best-selling gospel record of its time. In October 1950 she sang at Carnegie Hall in New York. In an age of segregation, Black and white listeners sat side by side to hear her. She later became the first gospel singer to win a Grammy.

She sang at benefit after benefit to raise money for civil rights. On August 28, 1963, at the March on Washington, a quarter of a million people gathered at the Lincoln Memorial. Before King spoke, she stepped up and sang a spiritual.

King began his speech reading from his notes. Then Mahalia called out to him: "Tell them about the dream, Martin!" A friend who was there said King pushed his notes aside and spoke from the heart: "I have a dream."

In 1968, King was killed, and she sang at his funeral. She died near Chicago on January 27, 1972, at the age of sixty, and tens of thousands of people came to her funeral. Today she is remembered as the Queen of Gospel. One honest shout can change a speech, and even an era.
