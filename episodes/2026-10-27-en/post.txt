Title: Paganini: 244 Years Ago Today, the Violinist Said to Have Sold His Soul Was Born

On October 27, 1782, Niccolò Paganini was born in the port city of Genoa, Italy. He played so brilliantly that people whispered he had sold his soul to the devil.

His father ran a small business by the harbour and played the mandolin. Niccolò learned the mandolin at five and the violin at seven. In 1794, not yet twelve, he gave his first public concert, in a church in Genoa. At eighteen, he became first violinist of the Republic of Lucca.

In 1805, Napoleon's sister Elisa became ruler of Lucca, and Paganini became a violinist at her court. In 1807, for Napoleon's name day, he wrote a sonata played entirely on a single string, the G string. Others needed four strings. He needed only one.

In 1828 he set off from Vienna on a tour of Europe, and in 1831 he conquered Paris and London. His fingers moved impossibly fast, and some even claimed they saw the devil helping him play. A young pianist named Franz Liszt heard him and decided to become the Paganini of the piano.

His 24 Caprices are still one of the great tests for violinists. Liszt, Brahms and Rachmaninoff all built new works on the 24th. He died in Nice on May 27, 1840. He left his beloved violin, "Il Cannone" (the Cannon), made by Guarneri del Gesù in 1743, to his hometown. Today it is kept in Genoa's town hall, and the winners of the Paganini Competition get to play it.

People called him the devil's violinist. But what he really left us was a violin that could do anything.

His question for you: if you had only one string left, what would you play?
