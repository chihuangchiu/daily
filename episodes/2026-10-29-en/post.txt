Title: Joseph Pulitzer: 115 Years Ago Today, the Man Who Got 125,000 People to Save Lady Liberty

On October 29, 1911, Joseph Pulitzer died in Charleston, South Carolina. He was a great newspaperman, and the man who got about 125,000 people to pay for the Statue of Liberty's pedestal.

He was born in Hungary in 1847. At seventeen he sailed to America alone and joined the Union Army in the Civil War. After the war he reported for a German-language paper in St. Louis, and by 1878 he owned a newspaper of his own, the St. Louis Post-Dispatch.

In 1883 he bought the New York World from the financier Jay Gould. He wanted a paper that spoke for ordinary people, and its sales soared.

By 1884, France's gift, the Statue of Liberty, was ready, but there was no money for its pedestal. In 1885 Pulitzer launched a fundraising drive in the World and promised to print the name of every donor, whatever the gift. Most people gave a dollar or less, yet about 125,000 people raised more than $100,000. In 1886, Lady Liberty was unveiled in New York Harbor.

Later his eyesight failed until he was nearly blind. In his will he left money to Columbia University to found a school of journalism, and a prize. He died on October 29, 1911, at the age of sixty-four.

In 1917 the first Pulitzer Prizes were awarded, and they still honor great journalism today.
