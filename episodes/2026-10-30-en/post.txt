Title: John Adams: 291 Years Ago Today, the Lawyer Who Defended the Enemy

On October 30, 1735, John Adams was born in Braintree, Massachusetts. He became a Founding Father and the second president of the United States, but first he was the lawyer who defended the soldiers no one else would.

On March 5, 1770, British soldiers fired into a crowd in Boston and killed five people. The city was furious, and no lawyer wanted to defend them. A young John Adams took the case, because he believed everyone deserves a fair trial. He told the jury: "Facts are stubborn things." In the end, the captain and six of the eight soldiers were acquitted; two were convicted of manslaughter.

In the Continental Congress, Adams pushed hard for independence. He chose young Thomas Jefferson to write the Declaration and fought for it on the floor. On July 4, 1776, the Declaration was adopted.

In 1797 he became America's second president, and in 1800 the first president to live in the White House.

Later, he and Jefferson fell out over politics. In 1812 Adams wrote first, and their letters went on for fourteen years. On July 4, 1826, fifty years after the Declaration, Adams died at ninety. His last words are remembered as "Thomas Jefferson survives." He didn't know that Jefferson had died a few hours earlier.

The Massachusetts Constitution he drafted in 1780 is still in effect: the oldest written constitution still working anywhere in the world.

His question for you: if a whole city hated someone, would you still stand up for their fair trial?
