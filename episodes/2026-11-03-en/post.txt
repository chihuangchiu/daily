Title: Henri Matisse: 72 Years Ago Today, the Master Who Drew with Scissors Died

On November 3, 1954, Henri Matisse, the leader of the Fauves, died in Nice, France, at the age of eighty-four. In his last years, working from a wheelchair, he picked up scissors and turned painted paper into a new kind of art.

He began as a clerk in a law office. At about twenty, appendicitis kept him in bed for a long time. His mother brought him a box of paints, and he left the law behind to become a painter.

In 1905, he showed his work at the Autumn Salon in Paris, including a portrait of his wife in a hat. The colours were so bold that a critic called the painters "wild beasts", and the name stuck: Fauvism.

Later, he painted for decades in sunny Nice, and for a Russian collector he painted the huge Dance.

In 1941, in his seventies, he had major surgery. After that he mostly worked from a wheelchair or from his bed. He could no longer paint as before, so he picked up scissors, cut painted paper into leaves, stars and figures, and called it drawing with scissors. In 1947 he published a book of cut-outs called Jazz.

In his last years he designed a small chapel in Vence, from the stained glass to the wall drawings, and he called it his masterpiece.

His question for you: if one day you could no longer do what you love the old way, would you find a new way?
