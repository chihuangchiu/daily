Title: Howard Carter: 104 Years Ago Today, One Step Led to Tutankhamun's Tomb

On November 4, 1922, in Egypt's Valley of the Kings, Howard Carter's workers found a stone step buried under the sand. It led down to the tomb of the boy pharaoh Tutankhamun, hidden for more than three thousand years.

In 1891, Howard Carter arrived in Egypt, aged seventeen. He was not yet an archaeologist but an artist, hired to copy the paintings on tomb walls. Later he learned to excavate.

He believed the Valley of the Kings still hid a lost royal tomb: the tomb of Tutankhamun. Funded by Lord Carnarvon, he dug for years and found little. In 1922, Carnarvon wanted to give up, but Carter talked him into one last season.

On November 4, workers uncovered a stone step, and below it a staircase leading down to a sealed door. On November 26, Carter cut a small hole in the door and peered in by candlelight. Carnarvon asked, "Can you see anything?" Carter replied, "Yes, wonderful things!"

The tomb held over five thousand objects, most of them intact. In February 1923, he opened the burial chamber, and at its heart lay a coffin of solid gold. For nearly ten years, he photographed and recorded every object, one by one.

Carter died in London in 1939. Today, people from all over the world still line up to see these treasures.
