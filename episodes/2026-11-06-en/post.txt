Title: James Naismith: 165 Years Ago Today, the Man Who Invented Basketball Was Born

On November 6, 1861, James Naismith was born near Almonte, Ontario, in Canada. With two peach baskets, he invented a game the whole world plays today: basketball.

Orphaned as a boy, he was raised by his uncle. Later, he studied at McGill University in Canada, fell in love with sport and became a physical education teacher. In 1890, he moved to Springfield, Massachusetts, to teach at the YMCA training school.

New England winters are long and cold, and his students, stuck indoors, were bored. His boss gave him a challenge: in fourteen days, invent a new game that could be played indoors.

He asked for two boxes to use as goals, but the janitor brought two peach baskets instead. He nailed them to the gym balcony at each end, about three meters up, and wrote thirteen rules for the notice board.

In December 1891, eighteen students split into two teams of nine and tried to throw a ball into the high baskets. It was the first basketball game in the world. In 1898, he joined the University of Kansas and became its first basketball coach.

In 1936, basketball joined the Olympics for the first time, and at seventy-four, Naismith handed out medals in Berlin. He died in Lawrence, Kansas, in 1939. Today, from school gyms to street courts, people all over the world are shooting hoops.
