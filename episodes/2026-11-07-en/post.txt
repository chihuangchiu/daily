Title: Marie Curie: 159 Years Ago Today, the First Double Nobel Winner Was Born

On November 7, 1867, Marie Curie was born in Warsaw, Poland. She would become the first person ever to win two Nobel Prizes.

Warsaw was ruled by Russia, and its university barred women. So she and her sister studied at the secret Floating University, whose classes kept moving to stay hidden from the Tsar's officials. She then worked for years as a governess, and in 1891 she finally reached Paris to study.

There she married the physicist Pierre Curie. In 1898 they found two new elements: polonium, named for her homeland, Poland, and radium. In a leaky old shed, they processed a ton of ore for more than three years to extract a tenth of a gram of radium.

In 1903 she shared the Nobel Prize in Physics, the first woman ever to win a Nobel Prize. Three years later, Pierre died in a street accident in Paris. She took over his post and became the first woman to teach at the Sorbonne. In 1911 she won a second Nobel Prize, in Chemistry.

When the First World War began, she realised X-rays could help doctors find bullets in wounded soldiers. She built mobile X-ray cars, learned to drive, and took her seventeen-year-old daughter Irène to the front. Together they also set up two hundred X-ray rooms in hospitals.

In 1934, years of radiation exposure took her life. In 1995 she became the first woman laid to rest in the Panthéon in Paris for her own achievements.
