Title: Christiaan Barnard: 104 Years Ago Today, the First Heart Transplant Surgeon Was Born

On November 8, 1922, Christiaan Barnard was born in Beaufort West, a small town in South Africa's dry Karoo. Forty-five years later, he gave a dying man a brand-new heart.

His father was a poor preacher. He studied medicine in Cape Town and qualified as a doctor in 1946. In 1956, he went to the University of Minnesota to learn heart surgery and work on a heart-lung machine. Back at Groote Schuur Hospital in Cape Town, he spent years practising heart transplants on dogs.

On December 3, 1967, Louis Washkansky, a 55-year-old grocer, had a failing heart. A young woman was fatally hurt in a car crash, and her family agreed to give her heart. Barnard's team operated for about five hours, and the new heart began to beat in Washkansky's chest. It was the first human-to-human heart transplant in history.

Washkansky died of pneumonia eighteen days later. But Barnard did not give up: in January 1968, he gave Philip Blaiberg a new heart, and Blaiberg lived for more than a year and a half.

In 1983, arthritis forced him to stop operating, and in 2001 he died in Cyprus. Today, thousands of people a year receive a new heart.
