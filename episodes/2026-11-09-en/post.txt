Title: Hedy Lamarr: 112 Years Ago Today, Hollywood's Inventor Star Was Born

On November 9, 1914, Hedy Lamarr was born in Vienna, Austria. She became a Hollywood star, and she was also an inventor.

In 1933, at eighteen, a film made her famous overnight. That same year she married Fritz Mandl, a rich arms dealer who would not let her act again. At his dinners, she quietly listened to talk of weapons.

Later she fled her husband and reached London, where she met MGM boss Louis B. Mayer and took a new name: Hedy Lamarr. In 1937 she reached Hollywood, hailed as its most beautiful woman.

During the Second World War, radio-guided torpedoes were easy to jam. With the composer George Antheil, she had an idea: make the signal hop from frequency to frequency, kept in step by player-piano rolls across 88 frequencies, one for each piano key. On August 11, 1942, they received a US patent.

But the Navy did not use the invention during the war. Only in 1962, during the Cuban Missile Crisis, did American warships use a similar system. In 1997, the tech world finally honoured her, and in 2000 she died in Florida, aged 85. In 2014, she entered the National Inventors Hall of Fame.

Today, Bluetooth earbuds use the same idea: frequency hopping.
