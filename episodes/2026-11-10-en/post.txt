Title: Friedrich Schiller: 267 Years Ago Today, the Poet of Ode to Joy Was Born

On November 10, 1759, Friedrich Schiller was born in Marbach, a small town in Germany. His poem Ode to Joy is now sung all over the world.

At thirteen, the Duke sent him to a strict military academy in Stuttgart. He was forced to study law, then switched to medicine, and in 1780 he became an army doctor. But what he really wanted to do was write.

In secret, he wrote his first play, The Robbers. On January 13, 1782, it opened in Mannheim, and the theatre went wild. He had gone to see it without leave, so he got 14 days in jail and a ban on writing plays.

On September 22, 1782, during the Duke's grand festival, he and his friend Andreas Streicher fled Stuttgart and headed for Mannheim. He gave up a safe life to keep on writing.

In 1785, he wrote the poem Ode to Joy, with its famous line: all people will become brothers. Later, Beethoven set it to music in his Ninth Symphony. Schiller became a history professor and wrote William Tell. On May 9, 1805, he died in Weimar, at just 45.

In 1972, the melody became the anthem of Europe, and in 1985 the anthem of the European Union too. Today, the whole world sings Ode to Joy.
