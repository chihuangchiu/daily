Title: Elizabeth Cady Stanton: 211 Years Ago, She Wrote “All Men and Women Are Created Equal”

On November 12, 1815, Elizabeth Cady Stanton was born in Johnstown, New York. She went on to write one of the most powerful lines in history: all men and women are created equal.

Her father was a judge, and as a girl she read the law books in his office. She saw how unfair the law was to women, and vowed to change it.

In 1840 she married the abolitionist Henry Stanton, and the word “obey” was dropped from her wedding vows. On their honeymoon they went to the World Anti-Slavery Convention in London, where women delegates were refused seats just because they were women. There she met Lucretia Mott, and the two planned a meeting of their own.

On July 19, 1848, in the small town of Seneca Falls, New York, America's first women's rights convention opened. Echoing the Declaration of Independence, Stanton's Declaration of Sentiments said: all men and women are created equal. She also made the boldest demand of all: the right to vote for women. One hundred people signed the declaration.

In 1851 she met Susan B. Anthony. Stanton wrote the speeches, Anthony gave them, and they fought side by side for nearly fifty years. In 1869 they founded the National Woman Suffrage Association. Stanton died in New York in 1902, eighteen years before women won the vote.

In 1920, the Nineteenth Amendment was ratified, and American women could finally vote.
