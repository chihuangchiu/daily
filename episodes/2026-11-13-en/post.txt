Title: Rossini: He Wrote The Barber of Seville in Under Three Weeks

On November 13, 1868, the Italian composer Gioachino Rossini died in Passy, near Paris. An opera he wrote in under three weeks has now been sung for 210 years.

He was born on February 29, 1792, in Pesaro, a small town on Italy's Adriatic coast, on a leap day that comes only once every four years. His father played the horn and his mother was an opera singer, so he grew up backstage in the theatre.

In 1813, at just 21, he staged his opera Tancredi at La Fenice in Venice. The audience was spellbound, and soon his name was known all over Europe.

In 1816, he took on a new comic opera in Rome. Working day and night, he finished it in under three weeks: The Barber of Seville. At its premiere on February 20 at the Teatro Argentina, the crowd jeered and mishaps hit the stage. Yet soon it swept across Europe.

In 1829, he presented William Tell in Paris. He was only 37, yet he never wrote another opera; by then he had already written 39. He died on November 13, 1868, at the age of 76. In 1887 his remains were taken back to Italy and laid to rest in the church of Santa Croce in Florence.

Two centuries on, The Barber of Seville is still sung in opera houses all over the world.
