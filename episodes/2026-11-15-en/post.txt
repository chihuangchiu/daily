Title: O'Keeffe: One Painted Flower Sold for $44 Million

On November 15, 1887, Georgia O'Keeffe was born on a wheat farm near Sun Prairie, Wisconsin. She became known as the mother of American modernism.

She grew up on the farm, then studied art in Chicago and New York. In 1915, while teaching in South Carolina, she made a series of abstract charcoal drawings.

She mailed them to a friend in New York, who showed them to the photographer Alfred Stieglitz. In 1916, without asking her, he hung ten of them in his gallery, 291. The next year he gave her a first solo show, and in 1924 the two were married.

She painted flowers huge, filling the canvas, so that, as she said, busy people would stop and look. In the 1920s, those giant flowers made her a star of the New York art world.

In the summer of 1929, she went to paint in New Mexico and fell for its desert, cliffs and bleached bones. In 1946, the Museum of Modern Art in New York gave her a retrospective, its first for a woman artist. In 1949, she made New Mexico her home.

She died in Santa Fe on March 6, 1986, at the age of 98. In 2014, her painting Jimson Weed/White Flower No. 1 sold for $44.4 million, then an auction record for any woman artist.

Her question for you: if no one sees the world like you, would you still paint it?
