Title: Soichiro Honda: From Rejected Piston Rings to 100 Million Bikes

On November 17, 1906, Soichiro Honda was born in Shizuoka, Japan. His father was a blacksmith who also fixed bicycles, and he grew up among the workshop tools.

In 1922, at fifteen, he became an apprentice at the Art Shokai garage in Tokyo, and in 1928 he opened his own repair shop in Hamamatsu. In 1936 he began making piston rings, but his first batch for Toyota was rejected. He kept improving them until they passed. After the war, he fitted small engines onto bicycles.

In 1948 he founded the Honda Motor Company, and in 1958 it launched the Super Cub. By 2017, global production of the Super Cub series had passed 100 million units. In 1954 he vowed to race at the Isle of Man TT, and in 1959, on its first attempt, the Honda team won the manufacturers' team prize. He retired in 1973 and died on August 5, 1991, at the age of 84.

From a blacksmith's son to a world-famous engineer, he failed, and started again, over and over.
