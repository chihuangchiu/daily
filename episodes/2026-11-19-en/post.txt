Title: Lincoln: The Two-Minute Speech That Redefined America

On November 19, 1863, President Abraham Lincoln spoke for about two minutes at the dedication of the Soldiers' National Cemetery in Gettysburg, Pennsylvania.

That July, at the height of the American Civil War, two armies had fought for three days around the small town of Gettysburg. More than fifty thousand men were killed, wounded, captured or missing, and thousands of fallen Union soldiers were buried on the hillside nearby.

At the dedication, the main speaker, Edward Everett, talked for two full hours. Then Lincoln, aged fifty-four, stood up and spoke for about two minutes, fewer than three hundred words. He said the nation was conceived in liberty and dedicated to the proposition that all men are created equal; that the dead must not have died in vain; that the nation should have a new birth of freedom; and that government of the people, by the people, for the people, shall not perish from the earth.

He said the world would little note what was said there. Yet the speech became one of the most famous in history, and it is carved on a wall of the Lincoln Memorial in Washington. The next day, Everett wrote to Lincoln that he wished he had come as near the central idea of the occasion in two hours as Lincoln did in two minutes. In April 1865, soon after the war ended, Lincoln was assassinated, at the age of fifty-six.

Great words do not have to be long. In two minutes, he put a nation's ideals into words.
