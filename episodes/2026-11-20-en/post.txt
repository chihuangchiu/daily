Title: Hubble: The Man Who Proved the Universe Is Far Bigger | 137 Years Ago Today

On November 20, 1889, the astronomer Edwin Hubble was born in Marshfield, Missouri.

He studied math and astronomy at the University of Chicago, then went to Oxford as a Rhodes Scholar, where, to honor his father's wish, he studied law. Back in the United States, he taught high school and coached basketball, but he could never let go of the stars.

In 1917 he earned his PhD in astronomy, then served in the army in France during the First World War. In 1919 he joined Mount Wilson Observatory in California, home of the 100-inch Hooker telescope, then the largest in the world.

At the time, many astronomers believed the Milky Way was the whole universe. On the night of October 5, 1923, Hubble photographed the Andromeda nebula. He marked one star with an "N", thinking it was a nova. Then he crossed out the N and wrote "VAR!": it was a Cepheid variable star, whose flicker reveals its distance. His result put Andromeda about 900,000 light-years away, far beyond the Milky Way. It was another galaxy. (Today we know it is about 2.5 million light-years away.)

On January 1, 1925, his discovery was presented at a meeting of the American Astronomical Society, and the universe suddenly held countless galaxies. In 1929 he showed that the farther away a galaxy is, the faster it moves away from us: key evidence that the universe is expanding.

Hubble died in 1953, at the age of sixty-three. In 1990, the Hubble Space Telescope was launched in his honor, to look deeper into space than ever before.
