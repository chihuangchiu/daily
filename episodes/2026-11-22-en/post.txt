Title: Abigail Adams: The Letter That Said "Remember the Ladies" | 282 Years Ago Today

On November 22, 1744, Abigail Adams was born in Weymouth, Massachusetts.

A minister's daughter, she never went to school, yet she taught herself by reading the books at home. In 1764 she married a young lawyer, John Adams. Over their lives, the two of them wrote more than a thousand letters to each other.

During the Revolution, John was away for years at the Continental Congress. Abigail ran the farm, the household money and the children on her own. In June 1775, holding the hand of her young son John Quincy on a hill near their home, she watched the smoke of the Battle of Bunker Hill.

On March 31, 1776, she wrote to John: "Remember the Ladies." She urged him not to put unlimited power into the hands of husbands, and warned that if women were ignored, "we are determined to foment a Rebellion," and would not be bound by laws in which they had no voice. John wrote back that he could not help but laugh.

In 1797 John became the second president of the United States, and Abigail became First Lady. In November 1800 she moved into the unfinished President's House, the first First Lady to live in the White House, and she hung laundry to dry in its empty East Room.

Abigail Adams died in 1818, at the age of seventy-three. Seven years later, her son John Quincy Adams became the sixth president. Her letters still let us hear the women of her time.
