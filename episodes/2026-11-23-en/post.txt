Title: Manuel de Falla: He Brought the Soul of Flamenco to the Concert Hall | 150 Years Ago Today

On November 23, 1876, the composer Manuel de Falla was born in Cádiz, a port city in southern Spain. Today marks the 150th anniversary of his birth.

His mother taught him piano, and he grew up hearing the folk songs of Andalusia. In 1905 his opera "La vida breve" (Life Is Short) won first prize in a composing contest in Madrid.

In 1907, with little money, he moved to Paris, where Debussy, Ravel and Dukas became his friends and urged him to write music that was truly Spanish. When the First World War broke out in 1914, he went home to Madrid.

In 1915 he wrote the ballet "El amor brujo" (Love, the Magician), with its "Ritual Fire Dance," as fierce as leaping flames and still a concert favorite. In 1919 his ballet "The Three-Cornered Hat" was premiered in London by the Ballets Russes, with sets and costumes by Pablo Picasso.

In the 1920s he moved to Granada, near the Alhambra. Worried that the ancient "cante jondo," or deep song, was dying out, in 1922 he and the young poet Federico García Lorca, among others, organized a deep song contest at the Alhambra to bring the old songs back.

In 1939 he left Spain for Argentina. He died in Alta Gracia in 1946, leaving his last large work, "Atlántida," unfinished. He showed the world that folk song can become great music.
