Title: Darwin: The Book That Changed How We See Life | 167 Years Ago Today

On November 24, 1859, Charles Darwin's "On the Origin of Species" was published in London.

In 1831, at twenty-two, Darwin boarded the survey ship HMS Beagle for a voyage of nearly five years around the world. In 1835 the ship reached the Galápagos Islands in the Pacific, where he collected giant tortoises and many kinds of finches.

Back in England, working with experts on his specimens, he saw that the finches' beaks differed from island to island. Slowly he realized that living things change over generations, and that those best suited to their surroundings leave more offspring: natural selection. Fearing the storm the idea would cause, he kept it hidden for nearly twenty years while he gathered more evidence.

In 1858 a letter arrived from a young naturalist in Southeast Asia, Alfred Russel Wallace, describing almost the same theory. That year their papers were presented together at the Linnean Society in London, and Darwin finally decided to finish his book.

On November 24, 1859, "On the Origin of Species" came out. All 1,250 copies of the first printing were quickly snapped up by booksellers. Some readers were amazed, others furious, and the world argued over where life comes from.

Darwin died in 1882 and was buried in Westminster Abbey. Today, evolution is the foundation of biology, and he taught us to trust the evidence.
