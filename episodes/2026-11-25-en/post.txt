Title: Carl Benz: He Built the First Car, His Wife Took It on the Road | Born 182 Years Ago Today

On November 25, 1844, Carl Benz, the inventor of the motor car, was born in Mühlburg, today part of Karlsruhe, Germany.

His father, a locomotive driver, died when Carl was two. His mother saved every coin to keep him in school, and at fifteen he entered the Karlsruhe Polytechnic, dreaming of a carriage that moved without horses.

His business struggled and money ran short, so his wife Bertha invested her dowry. On New Year's Eve 1879, his engine finally roared to life. On January 29, 1886, his three-wheeled motor car received a German patent, often called the "birth certificate" of the automobile.

But people laughed at the strange machine, and few wanted one. In August 1888, Bertha took their two teenage sons and, without telling Carl, drove to her mother's home, from Mannheim to Pforzheim, about a hundred kilometres.

When the fuel ran out, she bought a cleaning solvent called ligroin at a pharmacy in Wiesloch. When a fuel line clogged, she cleared it with her hatpin. That evening she arrived safely and sent Carl a telegram. Now all of Germany knew: the horseless carriage worked!

Carl Benz died in 1929 in Ladenburg, at eighty-four. Today, over a billion cars fill the world's roads, all starting from that little three-wheeler.
