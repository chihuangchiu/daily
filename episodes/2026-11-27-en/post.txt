Title: Anders Celsius: His Temperature Scale Started Upside Down | Born 325 Years Ago Today

On November 27, 1701, the Swedish astronomer Anders Celsius was born in Uppsala.

His grandfathers and his father were university scholars, and as a boy he loved mathematics and the night sky. In 1730, at just twenty-eight, he became professor of astronomy at Uppsala University.

At the time, scientists were arguing over the true shape of the Earth. In 1736, Celsius joined a French expedition led by Maupertuis to Lapland in northern Sweden to measure the land. The result showed that the Earth is flatter at the poles, just as Newton had predicted. In 1741, he founded the Uppsala Observatory.

Thermometers back then used dozens of different scales. In 1742, Celsius proposed using two fixed points, the freezing and boiling points of water, with one hundred steps between them. But he set boiling at zero and freezing at one hundred, so the hotter it got, the smaller the number!

He died of illness in 1744, aged only forty-two. Soon after his death, the scale was flipped: zero for freezing, one hundred for boiling. In 1948, it was officially named after him: degrees Celsius.

Weather forecasts, fevers, boiling water: they all use his hundred steps.
