Title: Christian Doppler: Why an Ambulance Siren Changes Pitch | Born 223 Years Ago Today

On November 29, 1803, the physicist Christian Doppler was born in Salzburg, Austria.

He came from a long line of stonemasons, but he was too frail for the family trade, so his father sent him to school. He studied mathematics in Vienna, yet for years he could not find a permanent teaching post. In 1835 he sold most of his belongings and set out to emigrate to America; just then, a teaching job in Prague came through, and he stayed.

On May 25, 1842, he presented a paper in Prague, "On the coloured light of the double stars and certain other stars of the heavens." He argued that when a source of waves moves toward you, the waves bunch up and the frequency rises; when it moves away, they stretch out and the frequency falls. We now call this the Doppler effect.

In 1845, the Dutch scientist Christoph Buys Ballot tested the idea near Utrecht. Trumpet players on an open railway car held a single note. As the train approached, listeners heard the note rise; as it left, the note fell.

In 1850 Doppler became the first director of the new Physics Institute at the University of Vienna. His lung disease grew worse, and he died in Venice on March 17, 1853, aged just 49.

Today, weather radar, police speed guns, medical ultrasound and astronomers measuring the motion of stars and galaxies all rely on his idea.
