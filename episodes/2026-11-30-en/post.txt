Title: Mark Twain: He Came In With Halley's Comet, and Left With It | Born 191 Years Ago Today

On November 30, 1835, the American writer Mark Twain was born in the village of Florida, Missouri. That year, Halley's Comet was passing close to Earth.

His real name was Samuel Clemens, and he grew up in Hannibal, a small town on the Mississippi River. When he was eleven, his father died, and he went to work as a printer's apprentice. Whenever a steamboat docked, the whole town ran down to see it, and his biggest dream was to become a steamboat pilot. In 1859, at twenty-three, he earned his pilot's license.

When river crews measured the depth, they called out "mark twain": two fathoms, about twelve feet, safe water for the boat. In 1863 he took that call as his pen name.

In 1876 he published The Adventures of Tom Sawyer, and later Adventures of Huckleberry Finn, in which the boy Huck and Jim, a man escaping slavery, drift down the Mississippi on a raft. Near the end, Huck decides, "All right, then, I'll go to hell," and chooses to help his friend reach freedom. Ernest Hemingway later wrote that all modern American literature comes from that one book.

In 1894, bad investments left him bankrupt. Nearly sixty, he did not run from his debts. He set off on a lecture tour around the world, and a few years later he had paid back everything he owed.

In 1909 he said he had come in with Halley's Comet and expected to go out with it. In April 1910 the comet returned to its closest point to the Sun, and he died the next day, April 21. Today his books are read in many languages, and they still make readers laugh.

His question for you: If you alone saw an injustice, would you stand up?
