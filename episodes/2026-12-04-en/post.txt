Title: Wassily Kandinsky: The Painter Who Heard Colours | Born 160 Years Ago Today

On December 4, 1866, Wassily Kandinsky, a pioneer of abstract art, was born in Moscow, Russia.

He studied law and economics at Moscow University and stayed on to teach there. In 1896 the University of Dorpat in Estonia offered him a professorship, but at thirty he turned it down, moved to Munich in Germany, and started over, learning to paint.

He later wrote that while hearing Wagner's opera Lohengrin at the Bolshoi Theatre in Moscow, he saw all the colours in his mind; that same year, Monet's Haystacks enchanted him. To him, colours could sing, and shapes had voices of their own.

He also told how, one evening at dusk, he walked into his studio and saw an indescribably beautiful painting made only of shapes and colours. Up close, it was one of his own paintings, turned on its side. He realized that a painting did not have to show any object at all.

In 1911 he wrote Concerning the Spiritual in Art, arguing that art should express a person's inner life, and that same year he formed The Blue Rider with Franz Marc and others. He began painting only lines, circles and patches of colour, becoming one of the first abstract painters. From 1922 he taught at the Bauhaus, teaching students with circles, triangles and squares.

In 1933 the Nazis closed the Bauhaus, and he moved to the outskirts of Paris. In 1937 the Nazis labelled his work "degenerate art" and removed it from German museums. He died in Neuilly-sur-Seine, near Paris, on December 13, 1944, at the age of seventy-seven. Today his paintings hang in museums around the world, and his ideas have shaped posters, design and countless artists.

His question for you: When you listen to music, what colours do you see?
