Title: Mozart: 35 Years of Life, Over 600 Works | Died 235 Years Ago Today

On December 5, 1791, the Austrian composer Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart died in Vienna. He was only 35.

He was born in Salzburg on January 27, 1756; his father, Leopold, was a court musician. Mozart was composing little pieces at five, and from the age of six his father took him and his sister Nannerl on tours of royal courts across Europe. With a cloth laid over the keys, he still played without a mistake, astonishing the nobles. At eight, in London, he wrote his first symphony.

In 1770, at fourteen, he heard Allegri's Miserere in the Sistine Chapel in Rome. The piece was sung only in the chapel and was not allowed to be copied. Back at his lodgings, he wrote the whole work down from memory.

As a young man he worked for the Archbishop of Salzburg, who often treated him like a servant. In 1781 he quit and moved to Vienna, where he no longer served a single patron but lived by teaching, giving concerts and writing operas: a daring choice in his time.

In Vienna he wrote operas such as The Marriage of Figaro and Don Giovanni. In September 1791, The Magic Flute premiered to packed houses. That same year, an anonymous patron commissioned a Requiem, but before he could finish it he fell ill, and he died in Vienna on December 5.

The unfinished Requiem was later completed by his student Süssmayr. Mozart left more than 600 works. Today his music is played all over the world, and the Voyager Golden Record, launched in 1977 and now beyond our solar system, carries an aria from The Magic Flute.
